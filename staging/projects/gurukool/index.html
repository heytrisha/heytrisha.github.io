Gurukool

An education app giving parents guided homework support, a peer community, and instant AI answers.

EdTechMobile AppCommunityAI Assistant
TL;DR — the 30-second version↓
  • Gurukool is an AI-assisted homework companion for parents who feel out of their depth helping their kids learn.
  • The problem
  • Parents no longer remember the concepts, teaching methods have changed, and existing edtech is built for students: help is scattered across videos, tutors, and WhatsApp groups.
  • Key challenges
  • Designing for users who feel inadequate: building their confidence instead of exposing the gap.
  • Earning trust in AI: parents are open to it only when it explains instead of just answering.
  • Consolidating a fragmented journey: learning, tracking, and community lived in separate tools.
  • Serving two audiences: parents first, with a lighter layer for students.
  • Key decisions
  • Community support: topic-based discussion spaces so no parent feels alone in the journey.
  • Personalised experience: content tailored to curriculum, grade, and progress so help is always relevant.
  • Trustworthy AI: GURU explains the reasoning instead of just giving answers.
  • One unified platform: Home, Curriculum, Guided Help, Community, and Tools in a single navigation model.
  • Solution
  • A parent-first app with guided video help, a curriculum tracker, a parent community, a quick-access toolkit, and GURU, an AI assistant that checks homework from a photo or voice note.

Challenges

What parents told

Parents want to help. But the way children learn today is different from how they were taught. They feel stuck, uncertain, and alone.

Research

Listening before designing.

To understand parents’ and students’ homework habits, challenges, and expectations, I used two key research methods.

User interviews

Interviewed 10 working and stay-at-home parents to better understand challenges, behaviors, and needs when supporting their children with schoolwork.

Key questions asked

  1. 01 ·

    Can you walk me through a typical day when your child has homework?

  2. 02 ·

    What kinds of subjects or topics do you find easy or difficult to help with?

  3. 03 ·

    What challenges do you face when supporting your child with homework?

  4. 04 · How much time do you spend helping with homework?
  5. 05 ·

    Have you ever felt stuck or unsure about how to explain a concept?

  6. 06 · What tools do you currently use?
  7. 07 · Would you trust AI-based homework help?
  8. 08 ·

    Are there any emotional challenges, like stress or guilt, during homework time?

  9. 09 ·

    Do you ever look for outside help (online resources, tutors, other parents)?

  10. 10 ·

    Have you ever wanted to connect with other parents or experts for advice?

  11. 11 ·

    What would an ideal support system or community for parents look like to you?

Competitive analysis

Analyzed 4 popular homework and learning support platforms (BYJU’S, Brainly, Toppr, and Vedantu) to understand existing solutions, identify feature gaps, and uncover opportunities to design a more parent-inclusive, guided learning experience.

Key findings

What was discovered

“Parents face time constraints and low confidence when helping with homework.”

“They prefer quick, reliable support over complex solutions.”

“Learning is fragmented across multiple tools, leading to lost progress.”

“Existing platforms are student-centric, with minimal parent support.”

“Trust in AI depends on clarity, transparency, and ease of use.”

The solution

Guided help, AI support, and a community, in one place

Gurukool replaces the chaos of searching online with a structured, guided experience. Parents start at Home, where they see recent activity, community updates, and quick access to their most-used tools. When homework gets tough, Guided Help offers topic videos, practice sheets, and step-by-step explanations. The AI assistant answers instantly through scans or voice notes. No more searching for trustworthy sources. And the Community connects parents who share the same struggles, so no one feels alone.

Structured over scattered

Everything organized into Home, Guided Help, Community, and Tool Kit.

Guided over guessing

Step-by-step explanations so parents never feel stuck.

Instant over searching

AI answers in seconds via scans or voice.

Community over isolation

Parents connect and learn from others facing the same challenges.

The thinking

Help at 8:14pm.

Parents aren’t asking for a better search engine. They’re asking for help at 8:14pm, when their child is staring at a textbook problem neither of them can crack, and the YouTube autoplay has cycled through four explanations that don’t match. That is the moment Gurukool is designed for. The moment a parent needs an answer, an explanation, and the reassurance that someone else has been there.

The product replaces scattered searching with a single trustworthy space: a home, a guided help layer, a community of parents in the same boat, and a tool kit for the everyday friction. The bet is that structured guidance outperforms infinite options for parents who already feel overwhelmed.

Key learnings

What this project taught me.

Balance Both Audiences

Effective solutions require a balance between parent usability and child engagement.

Empathy Drives Design

Empathy-driven insights lead to more meaningful and relevant design decisions.

Small Sessions, Real Signal

Walking interview participants through even a static prototype surfaces navigation and comprehension issues worth fixing.
