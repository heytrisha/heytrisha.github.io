Sampatti

Designing for the 40 million first-time investors in India who never make their first investment. A 2026 fintech case study.

Product design Fintech India market User research Information architecture

“Every major Indian investment app is designed for the moment after the decision to invest. Sampatti designs for the moment before it.”

40 million first-time investors entered the Indian market between 2020 and 2023. The apps are polished, the intent is real, and the infrastructure exists. Yet most of them never make their first investment. This case study is about why. And what happens when you design for the user who is still deciding.

The Problem

40 million first-time investors. Most never make their first investment.

The apps are polished. The intent is real. The infrastructure exists. Yet the gap between knowing and doing keeps widening.

The gap is not knowledge. It is confidence.

Every major Indian investment app is designed for the moment after the decision to invest. None are designed for the moment before it. Users arrive informed, ready, and find nothing that helps them cross the threshold with personalised guidance.

Who this product is for

Rohan Mehta, 23, Software developer, Bangalore. 8 months into his first job. ₹42,000/month take-home. Has watched 50+ SIP videos, downloaded Groww, completed KYC, but closed the app when he saw 200 fund options.

He is not financially unaware. He is aware but inactive, stuck at the decision threshold, not the knowledge threshold. He needs personalised guidance, not overwhelming options.

The Solution

Sampatti is built for the user who is still deciding.

Sampatti replaces the overwhelming fund catalogue with a single personalised recommendation based on two questions about your financial situation and risk tolerance. The first SIP starts at ₹100, pause anytime, stop anytime, no exit fees. Every term is explained in plain language where it appears, not buried in a glossary. Projections are calculated from your actual income and age, not generic calculators. And the guidance adapts as you invest, growing with you from first SIP to multiple goals.

The thinking

The decision is the hard part.

The decision to invest has always been treated as a knowledge problem. It isn’t. Forty million Indians are watching the videos, downloading the apps, completing the KYC. And walking away. What stops them is a missing moment of personalised guidance in the half-second after intent and before commitment, where every existing app hands them a catalogue instead of a recommendation.

Sampatti’s job is to fill that half-second. One recommendation. ₹100 to start. Plain language where terms appear. Projections that use your income and age. The work proves a single decision can reframe how an entire product category addresses its hardest moment.
