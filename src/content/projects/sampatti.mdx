{/* Design principles */}

<section class="mb-20">
  <span class="text-[color:var(--case-accent)] text-sm font-medium tracking-wider uppercase">Design principles</span>
  <h2 class="text-3xl md:text-4xl font-bold mt-4 mb-8">Five decisions that shaped every screen.</h2>

  <div class="not-prose flex flex-col gap-6">
    <div class="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.02] p-6">
      <h3 class="mb-3 text-lg font-semibold text-[#fafafa]">Start with intent, not instrument</h3>
      <p class="text-foreground/70 text-sm leading-relaxed">The experience is anchored on the user's job-to-be-done — "I want a home" — not the financial instrument, "I want a Large-Cap Index Fund." Fund selection only surfaces after the user has already made an emotional commitment to an outcome. This inverts the standard fintech discovery model (browse, filter, hope you find purpose) and uses a goal-first architecture instead, which reduces the abstraction gap between the user's mental model and the product's structure.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.02] p-6">
      <h3 class="mb-3 text-lg font-semibold text-[#fafafa]">Opinionated defaults with escape hatches</h3>
      <p class="text-foreground/70 text-sm leading-relaxed">The app recommends an 80/20 allocation based on timeline, but never locks it. A slider and preset chips allow override from 0/100 to 100/0. This balances cognitive ease for beginners (who need guidance) with agency for informed users (who want control). The default is derived from logic — time horizon plus risk profiling — but the interface makes override feel like a first-class feature, not a hidden setting.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.02] p-6">
      <h3 class="mb-3 text-lg font-semibold text-[#fafafa]">Curated discovery over infinite browse</h3>
      <p class="text-foreground/70 text-sm leading-relaxed">The app shows three funds per category — one recommended, two alternatives — rather than an exhaustive marketplace. This is a deliberate choice-architecture decision. Hick's Law predicts that more options increase decision time and decrease satisfaction. By curating direct-plan index funds with transparent selection criteria (expense ratio, consistency), it removes noise without removing agency. The "Recommended" badge is backed by a rationale, not an algorithmic black box.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.02] p-6">
      <h3 class="mb-3 text-lg font-semibold text-[#fafafa]">Radical transparency as a trust pattern</h3>
      <p class="text-foreground/70 text-sm leading-relaxed">Treating friction as content, not an edge case to bury. Exit loads, tax implications, and assumed rates are surfaced at the point of decision, not hidden in scheme documents. Most financial products use security theater — lock icons, "100% safe" badges — to build trust. This product has information honesty instead. Every fund card shows the exit load. Every graph carries its assumptions. This follows the progressive disclosure model: the user sees what they need to decide, and can expand for deeper regulatory context.</p>
    </div>
    <div class="rounded-xl border border-white/10 bg-white/[0.02] p-6">
      <h3 class="mb-3 text-lg font-semibold text-[#fafafa]">One decision per surface</h3>
      <p class="text-foreground/70 text-sm leading-relaxed">No screen asks the user to optimize a portfolio before they understand what a SIP is. The flow is strictly sequential: Amount, Allocation, Funds, Review. This applies cognitive load theory and the wizard pattern to a traditionally dense domain. By isolating each variable to its own screen, it prevents decision paralysis. The user never faces a dashboard of 50 funds on day one; they face one question at a time, with contextual education.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

  <span class="text-[color:var(--case-accent)] text-sm font-medium tracking-wider uppercase">Accessibility and reflections</span>
  <h2 class="text-3xl md:text-4xl font-bold mt-4 mb-8">The mindset behind the design.</h2>

<h3 class="mb-4 text-xl font-bold text-[#fafafa]">Accessibility and inclusion</h3>
<ul class="text-foreground/70 mb-10 space-y-3 leading-relaxed">
  <li>
    Dark mode chosen for visual calm in a finance context — the eye scans numbers, not lifestyle
    imagery, and a darker surface reduces competing visual weight on charts.
  </li>
  <li>Color is never the only signal: badges carry text labels alongside their tone.</li>
  <li>All interactive targets meet ≥44px touch.</li>
  <li>
    IBM Plex Mono is used for currency to prevent digit jitter during balance animations and to keep
    tabular figures aligned.
  </li>
  <li>
    Honest about what is not yet tested: keyboard-only navigation on the allocation slider,
    low-vision contrast between gold accent and dark-green surface, and screen-reader labeling for
    the projection graph data points.
  </li>
</ul>

  <div class="border-[color:var(--case-accent)]/30 bg-[color:var(--case-accent)]/5 rounded-xl border p-6 md:p-8">
    <span class="text-sm font-medium tracking-wider text-[color:var(--case-accent)] uppercase">Closing reflection</span>
    <div class="text-foreground/80 mt-4 space-y-5 leading-relaxed">
      <p>
        Designing Sampatti taught me that the hardest part of an investment app isn't picking funds — it's getting someone who downloaded an app, finished KYC, and still hasn't invested across the threshold. The gap is not financial literacy. It is decision confidence. Every screen in this case study is an answer to that one question.
      </p>
      <p>
        A note on where I started: I was new to finance when I began this project. I did not know enough about mutual funds, SEBI rules, or the Indian investment landscape to design for it credibly. So this was also a personal upskill — days of reading AMFI disclosures, scrolling through and reading about leading fin-tech apps in India, listening to investor podcasts, and asking people who actually invest what they wish their first app had done differently. The product you see here is the result of that learning.
      </p>
      <p>
        And to be clear about what this case study is not: it is not a finished product. There are screens I would redesign, assumptions I would test, and flows I would simplify further with real user feedback. The product will keep evolving.
      </p>
