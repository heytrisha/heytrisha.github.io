Sampatti

A fintech app helping first-time Indian investors cross the gap from intent to their first investment.

FintechMobile AppUX ResearchOnboarding

“Every major Indian investment app is designed for the moment after the decision to invest. Sampatti designs for the moment before it.”

The Problem

40 million first-time investors. Most never make their first investment.

The apps are polished. The intent is real. The infrastructure exists. Yet the gap between knowing and doing keeps widening.

The gap is not knowledge. It is confidence.

Every major Indian investment app is designed for the moment after the decision to invest. None are designed for the moment before it. Users arrive informed, ready, and find nothing that helps them cross the threshold with personalised guidance.

Who we’re designing for

Four segments. One deliberate focus.

Mapping the full landscape before narrowing — to show why choice matters.

1 · Not our user

The financially unaware

No working knowledge of financial concepts. Has not Googled SIPs or watched finance content. Needs foundational literacy before any investment tool is relevant.

2 · Adjacent, not our focus

The curious but conceptually shallow

Has heard of SIPs and mutual funds socially. Watched a few reels. Knows the vocabulary but not the logic behind it. Has never opened an investment app. Designing for them would recreate an educational product, a need already served by Varsity, Finology, and others.

3 · Our segment

The aware but inactive

Understands personal finance concepts. Has watched finance content, read articles, and researched platforms. Has downloaded an investment app and completed KYC, but has never made a single investment. Stuck at the decision threshold, not the knowledge threshold.

4 · Not our user

The active investor

Already has active SIPs or stock positions. Comparing fund performance, tracking XIRR, managing a portfolio. Well served by Zerodha, ET Money, and INDmoney.

Segment 3 was chosen because it has the clearest unmet need, the most designable problem, and the strongest business case. Users at this point have money and intent — the shortest conversion path — and need personalised guidance, not overwhelming options.

Software developer · Bangalore · 8 months into first job · Segment 3

₹42,000/month take-home · ₹8,000 disposable · 0 active investments

“I’ve watched like 50 videos on SIPs. I know I should start. I even downloaded Groww and completed the KYC. But when I saw all those fund options I just closed it. What if I pick the wrong one?”

Goals

  • Build a financial safety net without depending on parents
  • Feel financially capable, not just financially active
  • Take a first step that’s reversible

Pain points

  • 200 fund options, no guidance on which suits him
  • Fear of making a wrong, permanent decision
  • No product showed a personalised split based on his situation
  • Didn’t know if he should start with debt or equity

What he already knows

  • What a SIP is
  • That index funds outperform most active funds
  • That he should start early
  • That compound interest works in his favour at 23
  • Understands debt vs equity conceptually but doesn’t know how to split

What he’s tried

  • Googled “best SIP India”
  • Watched Groww tutorials
  • Downloaded Groww, completed KYC
  • Reached fund selection, saw 200+ options
  • Got overwhelmed, closed the app. Three times.
Design principles

Five decisions that shaped every screen.

Start with intent, not instrument

The experience is anchored on the user’s job-to-be-done — “I want a home” — not the financial instrument, “I want a Large-Cap Index Fund.” Fund selection only surfaces after the user has already made an emotional commitment to an outcome. This inverts the standard fintech discovery model (browse, filter, hope you find purpose) and uses a goal-first architecture instead, which reduces the abstraction gap between the user’s mental model and the product’s structure.

Opinionated defaults with escape hatches

The app recommends an 80/20 allocation based on timeline, but never locks it. A slider and preset chips allow override from 0/100 to 100/0. This balances cognitive ease for beginners (who need guidance) with agency for informed users (who want control). The default is derived from logic — time horizon plus risk profiling — but the interface makes override feel like a first-class feature, not a hidden setting.

Curated discovery over infinite browse

The app shows three funds per category — one recommended, two alternatives — rather than an exhaustive marketplace. This is a deliberate choice-architecture decision. Hick’s Law predicts that more options increase decision time and decrease satisfaction. By curating direct-plan index funds with transparent selection criteria (expense ratio, consistency), it removes noise without removing agency. The “Recommended” badge is backed by a rationale, not an algorithmic black box.

Radical transparency as a trust pattern

Treating friction as content, not an edge case to bury. Exit loads, tax implications, and assumed rates are surfaced at the point of decision, not hidden in scheme documents. Most financial products use security theater — lock icons, “100% safe” badges — to build trust. This product has information honesty instead. Every fund card shows the exit load. Every graph carries its assumptions. This follows the progressive disclosure model: the user sees what they need to decide, and can expand for deeper regulatory context.

One decision per surface

No screen asks the user to optimize a portfolio before they understand what a SIP is. The flow is strictly sequential: Amount, Allocation, Funds, Review. This applies cognitive load theory and the wizard pattern to a traditionally dense domain. By isolating each variable to its own screen, it prevents decision paralysis. The user never faces a dashboard of 50 funds on day one; they face one question at a time, with contextual education.

The Solution

Sampatti is built for the user who is still deciding.

Sampatti replaces the overwhelming fund catalogue with a single personalised recommendation based on two questions about your financial situation and risk tolerance. The first SIP starts at ₹100, pause anytime, stop anytime, no exit fees. Every term is explained in plain language where it appears, not buried in a glossary. Projections are calculated from your actual income and age, not generic calculators. And the guidance adapts as you invest, growing with you from first SIP to multiple goals.

Streak length before first missed SIP.

Accessibility and reflections

The mindset behind the design.

Accessibility and inclusion

  • Dark mode chosen for visual calm in a finance context — the eye scans numbers, not lifestyle imagery, and a darker surface reduces competing visual weight on charts.

  • Color is never the only signal: badges carry text labels alongside their tone.
  • All interactive targets meet ≥44px touch.

  • IBM Plex Mono is used for currency to prevent digit jitter during balance animations and to keep tabular figures aligned.

  • Honest about what is not yet tested: keyboard-only navigation on the allocation slider, low-vision contrast between gold accent and dark-green surface, and screen-reader labeling for the projection graph data points.

Closing reflection

Designing Sampatti taught me that the hardest part of an investment app isn’t picking funds — it’s getting someone who downloaded an app, finished KYC, and still hasn’t invested across the threshold. The gap is not financial literacy. It is decision confidence. Every screen in this case study is an answer to that one question.

A note on where I started: I was new to finance when I began this project. I did not know enough about mutual funds, SEBI rules, or the Indian investment landscape to design for it credibly. So this was also a personal upskill — days of reading AMFI disclosures, scrolling through and reading about leading fin-tech apps in India, listening to investor podcasts, and asking people who actually invest what they wish their first app had done differently. The product you see here is the result of that learning.

And to be clear about what this case study is not: it is not a finished product. There are screens I would redesign, assumptions I would test, and flows I would simplify further with real user feedback. The product will keep evolving.
